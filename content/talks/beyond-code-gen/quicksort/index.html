The PM Analogy: Sorting Feature Requests

Imagine you have 100 disorganized feature requests. Sorting them one by one is exhausting.

Instead, you pick one random feature (the "Pivot") to act as your benchmark. You ask your team a simple question for every other feature: "Is this more urgent or less urgent than our benchmark?"

You now have two smaller piles: Urgent and Not Urgent. You simply repeat this exact process on those smaller piles until everything is perfectly prioritized. That is Quicksort!

Why is this so fast?

If you have 100 features and you compare every single feature to every other feature to figure out their exact order, you'd have to make 10,000 comparisons (100 × 100).

But with Quicksort's "Divide and Conquer" approach, every time you pick a benchmark, you cut the remaining pile of work roughly in half! You only ever sort the smaller piles. This means you only have to split the piles about 7 times (since 27 ≈ 100). That's only ~700 comparisons instead of 10,000.

In computer science, this difference is known as O(N log N) vs O(N²). At massive scale (like millions of items), this is the difference between a task finishing in seconds versus taking thousands of years!
